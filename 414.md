```Question
Beethoven
Historical Profile
Illustration of Ludwig van Beethoven
German pianist and composer Ludwig van Beethoven (1770–1827) is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most innovative composers of all time, pushing the limits of classical forms and instrumental ranges well beyond what most musicians of his day could imagine. His alcoholic and abusive father, who was a court singer, taught him to play the piano through methods such as beating him each time he made a mistake. As an adult, Beethoven studied with Mozart and Haydn and became a prominent court musician in Vienna and Bonn. At the time of some of his greatest compositions, however, his hearing began to deteriorate, a fact that he tried to hide from others.
Beethoven was profoundly deaf by his late 20s and suffered periods of darkness, loneliness, and misery. He self-medicated with alcohol and opium and frequently isolated himself because of his difficulty in communicating. Accounts of his irritability over his condition mention arguing with his brother and throwing objects at his servants, and yet, he wrote that "art alone deterred" him from ending his life. His enormous body of work over the next quarter of a century reflects both his desperation and determination in every note. Tragically, Beethoven could not hear his own masterpieces being performed, including perhaps his most groundbreaking work, the Ninth Symphony ("Ode to Joy"). After the premiere performance, he remained facing the orchestra and could not hear the thunderous applause of the audience. A musician tapped the deaf composer's arm and asked him to turn around so that he could see the crowd's response. Beethoven died of complications from his chronic, heavy alcohol use at the age of 58.

Beethoven's irritability and restlessness may reflect which feature of depression?
    a. Anxious distress
    b. Pure dysthymia
    c. Rumination
    d. Somatic disturbance
```
```Answer
a
```

Photo of Mariah Carey
Mariah Carey is a singer, songwriter, record producer, and actor who has sold over 220 million records worldwide. In 2001, Carey was hospitalized for what was then termed exhaustion.     b. She has had at least one major depressive episode and at least one hypomanic episode.
